Here we are, in the strange, quiet days between Christmas and New Year's. The wrapping paper's in the trash, the calendar's almost out of pages, and most of us are doing a little looking back and a little looking ahead. It's a good instinct. Scripture does both.

We've spent two months on gratitude, generosity, and the coming of Jesus. I want to end the year by gathering all of that into one word: faithfulness. Finishing well.

## Look back with thanks

Before you rush into resolutions, stop and look back with gratitude. Whatever this year held, joy or grief, gain or loss, God was in it. He was faithful in the good stretches and he was faithful in the hard ones, even when you couldn't feel it. Naming that is how a Christian closes a year, not with a performance review, but with thanks. Count the grace. You'll find more of it than you remembered.

## Don't carry what you can't fix

A year always leaves some things unfinished, regrets, failures, prayers that haven't been answered yet. You don't have to drag all of that across the finish line. Paul, near the end of his life, said something worth borrowing: "Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal to win the prize for which God has called me heavenward in Christ Jesus."

Forgetting what is behind. Not pretending it didn't happen, but refusing to be chained to it. In Christ, last year's failures don't get to define next year. Grace lets you set the weight down and keep walking.

## Finishing well is really about pressing on

Notice Paul doesn't say "finish strong," as if it's all willpower. He says "press on," and he tells us why: because there's someone out ahead of him, calling him forward. Finishing well isn't about gritting your teeth into the new year with a longer list of rules. It's about following Jesus into the next stretch of road the same way you followed him through this one, one step at a time, leaning on his Spirit and not your own strength.

That's the whole Christian life, really. Not a sprint you win by trying harder, but a walk you finish by staying close to him.

## A word for the year ahead

So as this year closes, here's my prayer for you: that you'd end it grateful, travel light, and step into the new one following Jesus more closely than before. Whatever you're walking into, healing you're still waiting on, a decision you're dreading, a hope you're afraid to name, you don't walk in alone. The God who came near at Christmas doesn't leave in January.

## Come and see

If the new year finds you wanting to start following Jesus, or start again, there's no better time and no better company. We're a young church learning to walk with him together, and there's room for you in it.
