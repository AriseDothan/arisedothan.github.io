We've talked about a grateful heart, and about open hands. Now let's talk about where those open hands are pointed. Because generosity that only ever circulates inside the church family hasn't quite finished its journey. Real generosity moves out.

One of the three rhythms that shape life at Arise is what we call Moving Out: joining what God is already doing across our city. We believe he's at work in Dothan long before we ever show up, and our job is simply to get in on it. Some of that work looks like money. A lot of it looks like time, presence, and plain kindness.

## Love has an address

When Jesus was asked to name the greatest commandments, he gave two, and refused to separate them: love God, and love your neighbor as yourself. You can't have one without the other. Love for God that never reaches your neighbor is just talk.

And notice, he said neighbor, not "the deserving" or "the people like us." When someone tried to narrow the definition, Jesus told a story about a Samaritan who crossed every line to bandage a stranger, and then said, "Go and do likewise." Generosity with our city means our love has an address. It shows up on real streets, for real people, whether or not they can ever pay us back.

## The everyday kind

Most of this isn't dramatic. Being generous with Dothan looks like tipping well and speaking kindly to the person serving you. Learning your neighbors' names. Showing up when someone moves, grieves, or gets sick. Serving alongside the folks who are already loving this city, like the friends we partner with once a month to serve together.

You don't need a title or a budget for this. You just need to keep your eyes open and your hands loose. The kingdom of God usually advances through small, unglamorous acts of love that no one applauds.

## Why we bother

We're not generous with our city to earn anything, or to grow a crowd, or to look good. We do it because it's what Jesus did, he "went around doing good," and because we actually believe the good news is good. A church that hoards the grace it's received has misunderstood the grace it's received.

This is also the heart of being a Spirit-filled people. The same Spirit who fills us sends us. He doesn't fill us up so we can sit still; he fills us so we can pour out. Character and compassion were always meant to travel together.

## An invitation to join in

So here's a simple way to lean in this week. Ask God to show you one person or one need right in front of you, and then do something small about it. Don't overthink it. Generosity toward the city is learned the same way generosity of any kind is learned, one open-handed act at a time.

And when we serve together, there's a place for you. You don't have to be a member, or have it all together, or know a soul. Come lend a hand and love our city with us.

## Come and see

If you've been looking for a faith that actually does something, that gets its hands dirty for the good of the place you live, you've found some people who want the same thing. Come as you are, text Pastor Brian, and let's be generous with Dothan together.
