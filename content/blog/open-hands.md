Last week we started with a grateful heart, because gratitude is where everything begins. This week we take the next step, the one gratitude always leads to: open hands.

If you've spent much time around church, the word "generosity" might make you a little tense. You brace for the ask. So let me be clear from the start: this isn't a pitch, and it isn't a guilt trip. Generosity, the real kind, isn't something you get pressured into. It's something that spills out of you once you understand how much you've been given.

## We give because he gave first

The whole Christian story is a story of generosity, and God started it. He didn't hold back the thing most precious to him. Scripture puts it plainly: "For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you through his poverty might become rich."

That's the pattern. He emptied his hands so that ours could be filled. And when that truth moves from your head to your heart, tight fists start to loosen. You realize you're not the owner of your life; you're a steward of it. Everything you have is on loan from a generous God, and holding it loosely is just honesty about where it came from.

## Open hands are free hands

Here's something people rarely say out loud: greed is exhausting. Clutching, comparing, calculating, always afraid there won't be enough, it wears the soul down. Generosity is the way out. Not because giving earns you anything, but because open hands are free hands.

When you give, you're quietly declaring that God, not money, is your security. You're loosening money's grip on you. That's why Jesus talked about money so often, not because he wanted our stuff, but because he wanted our hearts, and he knew our wallets and our hearts are wired together.

## Cheerful, not cornered

Scripture is careful about the *how* of giving, not just the *whether*: "Each of you should give what you have decided in your heart to give, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver." Notice what God is after, not a bigger number, but a freer heart. He'd rather have a small gift given with joy than a large one squeezed out under pressure.

So generosity at Arise will never run on hype or manipulation. We're a Spirit-led people, and the Spirit doesn't strong-arm; he frees. If your giving is cheerful, keep going. If it's anxious, start smaller and let the joy catch up.

## Start where you are

Open-handed living is bigger than money, and it starts small. A meal for a neighbor. A tank of gas for someone stretched thin. A yes when you'd usually say you're too busy. Generosity is a muscle; it grows by being used.

And yes, it includes what we do with our finances. Giving to God's work isn't a fee the church collects; it's a joy the Christian gets to share in, a way of saying "this was never really mine, and I trust the one it belongs to." Take a step you can take cheerfully, and watch your heart follow your hands.

## Come and see

If money has had a grip on you, or generosity has only ever felt like pressure, there's freedom on the other side of open hands, and it's better than anything you're holding onto. You don't have to have it all figured out to begin. Come as you are, text Pastor Brian, and let's learn to live open-handed together this Sunday.
